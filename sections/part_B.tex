\section{ii) Second Dataset }
\subsection{a) Train LR model and perform grid search}
Cross-validation was used to jointly optimise the regularisation parameter C and the maximum polynomial degree. The mean 5-fold accuracy scores across the grid search are shown in a heatmap in Figure \ref{fig:d2_heatmap}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/d2_heatmap.png}
    \caption{Heatmap showing mean 5-fold cross-validation .}
    \label{fig:d2_heatmap}
\end{figure}

Model performance is consistently high across almost all tested configurations, with mean accuracy values above 0.97. Accuracy increases sharply between degree 1 and degree 2, after which improvements become negligible, indicating that most of the useful nonlinear structure is captured by the quadratic features. Variation across C values is minimal once C>2, confirming that the model's performance is not strongly dependent on the exact strength of regularisation.

The highest cross-validation accuracy of 0.981 is achieved at degree = 2 and C roughly = 20, though neighbouring settings yield nearly identical results. The heatmap indicates that this dataset contains classes that are highly separable and can well modelled by a low-order polynomial Logistic Regression classifier.

To further refine the choice of C, 5-fold cross-validation was repeated using the optimal polynomial degree (2). The results, shown in Figure \ref{fig:d2_c_plot}, display the mean classification error with error bars representing standard deviation across folds.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/d2_c_plot.png}
    \caption{Plot of CV error vs C parameter}
    \label{fig:d2_c_plot}
\end{figure}

The error decreases sharply for small C values and stabilises once C>10, with the minimum error of approximately 0.018 occurring at C=11.3. Beyond this point, performance remains relatively constant with small variability, indicating that the model's generalisation ability is robust to the exact value of C. These results are consistent with the heatmap findings, confirming that moderate regularisation achieves the best balance between bias and variance.


Figure \ref{fig:d2_db} shows the decision boundary of the optimised Logistic Regression model trained with polynomial features. Each point represents a training sample coloured by its true class, while the shaded regions indicate the predicted class.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/d2_db.png}
    \caption{Decision Boundary for Logistic Regression}
    \label{fig:d2_db}
\end{figure}

 The model clearly separates the two classes with a smooth, curved boundary, accurately capturing the nonlinear relationship between features. Only a small number of misclassified points appear near the decision boundary, confirming that the quadratic feature expansion was sufficient to model the data's underlying structure. This strong separation is consistent with the high cross-validation accuracy observed earlier, indicating excellent generalisation performance.

\subsection{e) Compare both models}

The confusion matrices (Figure \ref{fig:d2_cm}) show both trained models achieve near-perfect classification, with only a small number of misclassified samples. Logistic Regression correctly classifies over 98\% of instances, while kNN performs comparably with slightly fewer total errors. In contrast, the dummy classifier shows a roughly even distribution of predictions across both classes, reflecting random guessing. These results confirm that both models generalise extremely well on this dataset, consistent with the strong separation visible in earlier plots.

Figure \ref{fig:d2_roc} shows the ROC curves. Both trained models achieve near-perfect discrimination, with AUC = 0.998 for each, indicating almost complete separation between the two classes. The curves lie close to the top-left corner of the plot, confirming that both models maintain very high true positive rates while keeping false positives close to zero. The baseline classifier follows the logarithmic line (AUC=0.5), representing random guessing. These results are consistent with the confusion matrices and decision boundaries, demonstrating that the dataset is highly separable and that both models generalise exceptionally well.
